MOT Failure Rates by Car: How to Read the Data

AutoSafe's comparison artifact contains aggregated official MOT records grouped by model family, age band and mileage band. Those records can show how groups performed historically. They cannot establish the present condition or next result of one submitted vehicle.

Interpretation boundary: the 26.9% figure describes recorded tests in this dataset. The source-record coverage period is not established here. It is not a current annual UK rate, a percentage of unique cars, or a result for your car. A physical inspection and the official MOT are the only ways to assess its current condition and test outcome.

Why model, age and mileage matter

A model-wide average can hide material differences between newer and older vehicles. AutoSafe therefore starts with the narrowest supported group: model family, age band and available mileage band. If that cohort has no usable evidence, the report broadens to age-only, then model-wide, then the dataset reference, and labels the scope used.

Compare compatible groups

Recorded failure rates vary between groups. Compare figures only when the groups, coverage periods and test definitions are compatible. A group rate cannot establish the condition of your car.

Age-band and component tables are withheld while their definitions, numerators, denominators and coverage lack a documented review. Component categories may overlap within a test and should not be added as though they were exclusive. A source-file revision date does not establish the period covered by its records.

How to use a comparison responsibly

  1. Check the evidence scope and sample count before reading the percentage.
  2. Treat estimated mileage differently from an observed MOT odometer reading.
  3. Do not interpret component patterns as faults on the submitted car.
  4. Use GOV.UK's official service for the authoritative MOT history.
  5. Arrange a physical inspection if you need a condition assessment.

Methodology and provenance

The primary artifact is prod_data_clean.csv.gz. Its 254,145 cohort rows sum to 148,509,908 tests and 39,969,903 failures. The dataset-wide rate uses total recorded failures divided by total recorded tests. Those totals count tests, not unique vehicles. The underlying record coverage period is not established here. The release claim sweep recalculates the totals from the gzip CSV and fails if the contract metadata or public dataset-size claim drifts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which car has the lowest or highest MOT failure rate?

A durable ranking needs careful control for age, variant mix, sample size and data quality. Use the live make/model pages for current recorded cohorts; do not treat a low group rate as a condition assessment or buying recommendation.

Do older groups record more failures?

That requires compatible age groups, test definitions and coverage periods. Age alone does not determine the condition or next result of an individual vehicle.

Can AutoSafe predict whether my car will pass?

An AutoSafe report may contain a labelled model estimate, a group comparison, a dataset reference or unavailable evidence. None inspects the car or determines its next MOT result.
