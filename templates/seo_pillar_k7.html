What the report tells you

  • The vehicle identity and latest MOT details returned by the configured vehicle-data source.
  • Whether mileage came from your entry, an observed MOT reading, an explicit age-based estimate, or was unavailable.
  • The historical failure rate for the closest supported cohort, plus the cohort scope and sample count.
  • Component patterns and indicative cost context only when all required component evidence is present.

What it does not tell you

  • It does not inspect or diagnose the submitted vehicle.
  • It does not calculate a pass probability, reliability score or next-MOT prediction.
  • A component pattern does not establish that a fault exists on this car.
  • A lower group rate does not prove that this car is safe or roadworthy.

How the evidence ladder works

  1. Exact band: same model family, age band and available mileage band.
  2. Age only: same model family and age band when the mileage cohort has no usable evidence.
  3. Model average: the model family across age and mileage bands.
  4. Dataset reference: {{ '%.1f'|format(dataset_reference_rate * 100) }}% only when no model cohort is available, labelled as not vehicle-matched.

PostgreSQL and the SQLite fallback use the same sample-size-weighted cohort arithmetic. The report identifies which backing store answered so availability changes cannot masquerade as stronger evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AutoSafe guarantee whether my car will pass?

No. The comparison does not inspect the car and cannot determine its next MOT result.

Why can the comparison become less specific?

A narrower cohort is used only when it has usable evidence. Otherwise the report broadens the scope and says so, rather than inventing a precise-looking value.

Why might component and cost sections be absent?

They are withheld when the cohort does not contain complete component evidence. Missing detail is not treated as zero risk.
